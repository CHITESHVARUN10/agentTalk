How it works

Three steps. No settings to fiddle with.

The whole interaction is one key, twice.

Press ⌘⇧D

The glass pill appears wherever you placed it — bottom-center by default, or anywhere you like. A live waveform shows your voice being captured.

Speak

While you talk, AgentTalk transcribes in 20-second chunks on-device. A live preview grows above the pill, so you see the words as they're recognized.

Press ⌘⇧D again

The transcript appears, ready to copy. One more press closes it and starts a fresh recording — no mouse required.

What makes it different

Built like a utility, not a dashboard.

No signup. No onboarding quiz. No "welcome to your workspace."
01

Offline, by design

Speech is converted to text by a Whisper large-v3-turbo model running entirely on your machine — accelerated by Metal on Apple Silicon. There is no server to send audio to, because the app has no network features at all. Turn Wi-Fi off; it still works.

The microphone permission prompt says it plainly: "Audio never leaves your device." That's a promise the architecture keeps.

02

Words appear while you're still talking

AgentTalk doesn't wait until you stop. It transcribes the audio stream in 20-second chunks with a 2-second overlap, stitching the results with text-level deduplication so nothing is lost or doubled at the boundaries — and the preview bubble shows the transcript growing in real time.

If you stop early, the whole buffer falls back to a single clean pass. Short recording, long recording — no gaps either way.

03

The pill lives where you want it

By default the recording pill sits bottom-center. From the menu bar, pick "Custom position" and a dummy pill appears that you can drag anywhere on screen — no recording needed. Press the checkmark, and that spot becomes the pill's permanent home. Your placement is remembered across restarts.

If the pill sits near the top of the screen, the transcript smartly appears below it instead of floating off-screen.

04

Heavy when needed, light when idle

The 1.5 GB model downloads once, automatically, with progress shown in the HUD. After six minutes of silence it unloads from memory — freeing roughly two gigabytes of RAM — and reloads transparently when you next press the hotkey. Recordings made during a reload queue up and process without losing a word.

05

Launch at login — only if you ask

A menu bar toggle registers the app with macOS Login Items. It's off by default; the moment you switch it on, AgentTalk is ready at your desk every morning. Turn it off the same way, or from System Settings.

Privacy

No cloud. No account. No asterisk.

Most "on-device" products still phone home. This one has nothing to call.

What AgentTalk does

  • ✓Transcribes locally — audio is captured and decoded entirely on your Mac via Metal-accelerated Whisper.
  • ✓Keeps recordings ephemeral — audio lives in memory for the recording, then is discarded.
  • ✓Works offline — no Wi-Fi, no connection, no difference.

What AgentTalk does not

  • ✕No accounts — nothing to sign up for, nothing to log into.
  • ✕No telemetry — no analytics SDK, no crash reporters, no phone-home.
  • ✕No cloud storage of your words — transcripts exist on your clipboard and your screen, nowhere else.

The only network activity in the entire app is the one-time model download from Hugging Face. Everything after that is local.

FAQ

Honest answers

Is it really offline? Like, no internet at all?

Really. Audio is captured and transcribed by a Whisper model running on your Mac. The app contains no networking code for audio — the only network call in the whole program is the initial model download. With Wi-Fi off, dictation is unchanged.

Why does it download a 1.5 GB model on first launch?

The speech recognizer (Whisper large-v3-turbo) is a neural network that ships as a model file — not code. Bundling it would make the app download 1.5 GB for everyone. Instead, the app itself stays at ~5 MB, and the model is fetched once from Hugging Face, verified, and stored in ~/Library/Application Support/AgentTalk/models. No account needed.

Which languages does it support?

English only, for now. The pipeline is configured for English dictation. Other languages need code changes (and would still work offline) — a future release.

Why does macOS warn "unidentified developer" on first launch?

Because the app isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer ID certificate ($99/year). The warning is one-time: right-click the app in Finder → Open → Open. After that, no more warnings. If you build from source, there's no warning at all.

What happens to my audio and transcripts?

Audio exists only in memory while you record and is discarded when the recording ends. Transcripts go to your clipboard (and the transcript window). AgentTalk doesn't store your dictation history anywhere.

How do I uninstall?

Quit the app, delete AgentTalk from /Applications, and optionally remove ~/Library/Application Support/AgentTalk (which contains the downloaded model) and ~/Library/Preferences/com.agenttalk.app.plist. That's everything — there's nothing else on your system.
